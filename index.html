The Generation Gap Widens

Homeownership rates by age group · 2005–2024 · Source: U.S. Census Bureau HVS
Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Current Population Survey / Housing Vacancy Survey (CPS/HVS), 1994–2024
The gap is structural, not cyclical. While all age groups dipped during the 2008 foreclosure crisis, the under-35 cohort never fully recovered — and has trended down since 2020. Adults 65+ have stayed flat near 79%. The distance between these two lines has grown by nearly 12 points since 2005.

Among adults under 60, high earners own at more than 3× the rate of low earners. Meanwhile, the median first-time buyer is now 38 — eight years older than in 2010. The path to ownership has lengthened significantly, not just financially but in terms of life stage.
04 —

Why Renters Stay Renters

Self-reported reasons for renting rather than owning · 2024 · Federal Reserve SHED
Source: Federal Reserve Report on Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households (SHED) 2024
Key Finding
Over 1 in 5 renters say renting is simply more convenient — not a financial necessity.
Market Implication
PropTech and rental platforms should not assume their users are waiting to buy. A significant and growing share are long-term renters by choice — a segment with distinct product, service, and community needs.
05 —

The Cost of Staying Flexible

Percentage of renters spending 30%+ of income on housing · 2010–2023 · U.S. Census ACS
Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS); Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies, State of the Nation's Housing 2024
Rent burden hit a 15-year high in 2023. After briefly declining during the pandemic — partly due to relief programs and lower urban rents — the share of cost-burdened renters surged back above 50% as rents spiked in 2022–2023. This squeeze is paradoxically making it harder to save for a down payment, further extending rental tenure.
